# PatrolGrid Product

## Register

product

## Platform

android

## Users

Patrol personnel use PatrolGrid in the field during day beat, night, foot, vehicle, highway, and special patrol duties. Their primary task is to understand an assigned patrol mission, record reliable evidence while working online or offline, document operational deviations, and finish with a transparent summary of what the app recorded.

Subdivision supervisors use the same Android application through a role-specific Patrol Control experience. Their primary task is to assign patrol areas and priorities, coordinate active units, identify exceptions that need attention, and review completed patrols with the officer's operational context.

## Product Purpose

PatrolGrid replaces isolated location posts with structured patrol missions and trustworthy coverage evidence. A mission combines a duty window, patrol area, priority locations, an optional suggested route, and operational instructions. The application records location only during an explicit patrol session, works through weak connectivity, and distinguishes completed, partially completed, operationally diverted, technically inconclusive, and supervisor-review outcomes.

Success means supervisors can understand where patrol occurred and what needs attention without forcing personnel to follow a rigid route when field conditions require judgment.

## Positioning

Patrol accountability that supports field judgment: plan the beat, coordinate the patrol, and review coverage with context.

## Brand Personality

Calm, fair, and operational. PatrolGrid should feel like dependable duty equipment rather than a productivity tracker. Its language is direct and respectful, and its evidence states communicate uncertainty honestly.

## Anti-references

PatrolGrid must not resemble employee-surveillance software, a staff leaderboard, a delivery-driver tracker, or a punitive scorecard. It must not hide tracking, present approximate GPS as exact, or turn an unexplained data gap into an automatic misconduct conclusion.

## Design Principles

1. **Mission before movement.** Explain the patrol purpose, area, priorities, and time before showing tracking metrics.
2. **Exceptions before surveillance.** Patrol Control highlights safety events and missions needing attention instead of encouraging continuous observation.
3. **Evidence with context.** Coverage, checkpoint completion, GPS confidence, incidents, and officer explanations appear together.
4. **Field judgment remains human.** Routes are suggested unless the duty genuinely requires corridor coverage, and administrative conclusions require supervisor review.
5. **Visible, bounded tracking.** Personnel can always see when tracking is active, inspect their recorded route, and end the patrol session.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Follow Material 3 and Android accessibility conventions. All controls require 48 dp touch targets, scalable text, screen-reader labels, non-color status cues, and clear focus order. Daylight and night-duty use require strong contrast and an intentional dark theme. Reduced-motion settings are honored, and critical patrol actions remain usable with intermittent connectivity and one-handed interaction.
